If you sell half to the kids

Their money pays off the loan, you end up debt free, and the rest comes to you monthly, with interest. Everyone stays put. Co-owners pay no rent; costs split down the middle.
Your half stays fully yours, to live in and to leave however you wish.
The monthly payments are what is called seller financing: you are the bank, and the interest goes to you instead of one. The attorney records it against the kids' share of the property, the same protection a bank gets. If the payments ever stopped, that share would come back to you.

If you sell the whole place

The place becomes the kids'. The loan is paid off, you take the cash you choose, the rest comes monthly. You stay in your home and pay them a normal rent, which keeps it proper for taxes.
More cash now, smaller monthly checks later.
The monthly payments are what is called seller financing: you are the bank, and the interest goes to you instead of one. The attorney records it against the property itself, the same protection a bank gets. If the payments ever stopped, the property would come back to you.
