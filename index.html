If you sell half to the kids

Part of the kids' purchase money pays off the remaining bank loan, so you end up debt free. The rest of their half price comes to you as monthly payments, with interest. You are the bank. You both keep living exactly where you live, and because you are co-owners, nobody pays anybody rent. The running costs get shared down the middle.
Your half stays fully yours: to live in for as long as you want, and to leave to whomever you choose. This purchase does not touch your estate decisions about your own half.

If you sell the whole place

The whole property becomes the kids'. The bank loan is paid off, you take whatever cash you choose at closing, and the rest of the price comes to you as monthly payments. You stay in your home, and you pay the kids a normal monthly rent, the way any tenant would, which is what makes it proper for taxes.
On top of the loan being paid off. More cash now means smaller monthly payments later, since it comes out of the same price.

Good questions to ask

For an attorney, a CPA, or an AI assistant, in any order.

  • What is a fair interest rate for a family loan right now, and what is the IRS minimum this month?
  • What happens to a co-owned property when one owner passes away?
  • What should a written co-ownership agreement cover so nobody ever has to guess?
  • How would selling half, versus all, of our home affect our taxes specifically?
  • What should we ask a real estate attorney before signing anything?
